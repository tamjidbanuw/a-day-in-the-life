The story at 35 countries,
with life expectancy pulled out

Removing life expectancy and widening to 35 countries are the same edit. One line in js/app.js keeps only countries that have life expectancy, happiness and tourism, and that line is the entire reason the story follows twelve.

It is 34, not 35. Luxembourg has no World Happiness Report score in adl-data.js, so any chart gated on happiness drops it. Time use and tourism cover all 35. Below, each chart states its own n rather than the page forcing one number on all of them — which is the honest fix, but it does mean the country count changes between charts.

1 · The ranked bars, the chart most at risk

This is the Chapter One workhorse: paid and unpaid work stacked, countries ranked by total. At twelve it is a tidy block. At 35 it is three times as tall, and the question is whether a reader still reads it or just scrolls past.

2 · The slope chart, the chart most likely to break

Chapter Three ranks every country on five measures to show that no country leads on all of them. Life expectancy was one of the five, so it becomes four. Four measures still produce four different winners, and the new winners are the more recognisable ones.

Rank on four measures
Rank 1 is the most of that thing. Hover any line to follow one country through. Happiness is n=34, so the happiness column has one fewer rank than the other three.

Four Measures, Four Winners Bump chart, one line per country, rank on each of four daily measures. Lines are grey except the twelve the story currently follows.

Data source: OECD Time Use Database; World Happiness Report via data/adl-data.js.

Note: Ranking discards distance. Two countries a minute apart sit a whole rank apart here, which at 35 countries happens far more often than at twelve.

That chart does not work, and it is the clearest argument on this page for keeping something at twelve. Thirty-five lines crossing four columns is a texture, not a reading: the finding it exists to deliver — that no country leads everywhere — is buried by the very evidence meant to prove it. 3 · What fills the hole life expectancy leaves

Chapter Two was built on two ways of scoring a life: count the years, or ask. Take the years away and the chapter has one measure and no argument. The repo already holds a replacement it has never used — GDP per capita, present for all 35 countries — and it correlates with happiness harder than life expectancy ever did.

Money against mood
GDP per capita on a log scale against the happiness ladder, n=34.

The Measure That Actually Agrees One dot per country, GDP per capita (log scale) against the World Happiness Report ladder score. The line is the least-squares fit.

Data source: World Happiness Report and GDP per capita, both via data/adl-data.js.

Note: A log scale is used because a dollar matters less the more of them you have; on a linear axis the same relationship looks like a curve and reads as weaker than it is. Correlation is not cause in either direction.
