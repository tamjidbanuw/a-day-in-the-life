Meet the data

8 Billion People

Measuring a day takes a national diary survey. Thousands of people write down what they did and for how long. Only 35 countries run one.

This story follows the 12 with complete records. Between them they hold 3.82 billion people, 48% of everyone alive. Nine are high-income countries. The other three, India, China and Mexico, are not. Read this as a window on rich and emerging economies, not the world.

    The second shift

    Half your waking hours
    go to work

    Every day carries two workloads: the job you're paid for, and the cooking, cleaning and caring that no one puts on a payslip.

    Count both and Mexico works the longest day here, 10h05m. Canada is next at 9h02m, though more of its day is paid. Italy has the shortest paid day measured, 2h57m, and still does more unpaid work than paid.

    The other half of the day

    After the work is done, what's left is rest: the hours for family, friends, screens, sport and simply doing nothing.

    Chapter Two

    How We Thrive

    Effort is supposed to pay off. The numbers are less obedient than that.

    First measure

    Happiness is a question,
    not a reading

    Nobody measures happiness. They ask. Imagine a ladder where 10 is the best possible life for you and 0 the worst. Where do you stand today?

    Thousands of people in each country answer that, and the average is the score. It is a country's own opinion of itself, which makes it the softest number in this story and the only one that asks people directly.

    Happiness, 0 to 10

    Second measure

    Life expectancy is
    the opposite kind of number

    Nobody is asked anything. It is counted from deaths already recorded: how many years a baby born today can expect if nothing changes.

    So one measure is an opinion and the other is arithmetic. They are not the same question, and a country can do well on one and badly on the other. Read the two scales together and most countries hold roughly the same position in both.

    Nobody leads

    Five measures,
    five different winners

    Line up all twelve countries on all five measures and no single line stays on top. Mexico works the most. France sleeps the most. Germany rests the most. Japan lives longest. Australia is happiest.

    Same hours.
    Different lives.

    Everyone is handed the same 1,440 minutes. What changes, from Osaka to Mexico City to Rome, is what becomes of them.

    Work or rest. Paid or unpaid. Alone or together. How a country fills its day shapes how long its people live, how connected they feel, and how content they are to be here. Whose day would you want?
