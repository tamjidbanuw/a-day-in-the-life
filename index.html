04 — Rest

The other half of the day —
and who guards it

After the work is done, what's left is rest: the hours for family, friends, screens, sport and simply doing nothing.

Germany and Italy protect the most free time — over 5½ hours a day. At the other end, Mexico keeps barely 3½, its day already spent on work seen and unseen. The countries that work the hardest, unsurprisingly, rest the least.

05 — Thrive

A longer day doesn't
buy a better life

If hard work alone made life better, the busiest countries would be the happiest and longest-lived. They aren't.

Japan lives the longest (83.9 years) yet ranks mid-pack for happiness. Australia works far less than Japan but is the happiest here. And India, with a long working day, sits lowest on both. How a day feels depends on far more than how full it is.

06 — Connect

However we spend our days,
the world still comes to visit

For all our differences, we're endlessly curious about each other's lives — and hundreds of millions of us go and see them firsthand.

France alone welcomes nearly 218 million visitors a year — more than three times its own population. Wherever people live, and however they spend their 24 hours, the days of one country keep drawing the curiosity of another.

The same 24 hours.
A different life.

Everyone on Earth is handed the same 1,440 minutes each day. What changes — from Osaka to Mexico City to Rome — is how we spend them.

Work or rest. Alone or together. Seen or unseen. The way a country fills its day shapes how long its people live, how connected they feel, and how happy they are to be here. Whose day would you want to live?
