Chapter One as counted cells

One row per country, 48 cells per row, one cell per half hour. All 35 always on screen — nothing is ever behind a click — and the sort is what turns one chart into three sections.

This is the surviving idea from nine treatments, kept on its own so the next round starts from something that works rather than something to argue with. Its open problem is that it has no focal point: 1,680 squares of equal weight, no hierarchy, no annotation. That is a problem of direction, not of mark.

The day of 35 countries, in half-hours
One cell is 30 minutes. Sort to reorder; every country stays on screen.

Counted Cells Forty-eight cells per country, one per half hour of the average day, coloured by what the day is spent on. Cells are allocated by largest remainder, so every row sums to exactly 48 and a twenty-minute block still gets a cell instead of disappearing. Cell order is fixed and is not a timeline — the OECD data records how much, never when. Ranks are by size, not merit. OECD Time Use Database, both sexes, 35 countries.
