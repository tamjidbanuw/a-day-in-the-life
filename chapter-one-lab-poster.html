Thirty-five countries keep a time diary — a record of what thousands of people actually did across 24 hours. They are the only places on earth where an ordinary day has been written down and counted.

Everything here is one day. Hover any country, in the braid or the ranking, and every panel becomes that country; let go and it returns to the average of all 35. The one finding worth carrying out: the biggest block of the day is also the one nobody disagrees about.

How to read this

Three things that are easy to get wrong.

Order is not time. Every block and ribbon sits in a fixed order. The data records how much of the day went to something, never when — no country here is asleep at midnight because the grey band is on the left.

Every country counts once. The average is an average of 35 days, not of 4.2 billion people. China and Luxembourg carry the same weight, which is why the figures differ from a population-weighted world.

Ranks are size, not merit. First on work is the longest working day, not the best one. More sleep is not better than less.
